The problem

How much of this still runs on WhatsApp?

A practice your size already has a system. It's just spread across tools that don't talk to each other — and each one only answers the question you asked it at the time. That isn't disorganisation; those tools got you here.

The software that does exist was built for agencies and learning centres: desktop-first, admin-heavy, and it hands the student a portal no fifteen-year-old opens twice. So the tutor does the admin anyway, and the student stays on WhatsApp.

Why this one is different

Your students get a planner of their own — not a login to your admin system.

Planur started as a student planner: a teenager's own app for organising their week, their homework and their revision. Nothing to do with tutoring. Connect a student with a join code and your classes and materials land inside that — which is why the material you share actually gets read. It's sitting where they already look.

Said plainly. The student app is Testing — in final testing, not yet publicly released. It launches alongside this, and your students would be among the first on it. We'd rather tell you now than let you find out later.

What it does

The parts that cost you evenings.

Your classes and your register

Batches, sessions and attendance — marked in seconds, and still answerable six weeks later when a parent asks how many their child missed.

Materials, shared once

Upload a worksheet to a batch and everyone enrolled has it — including whoever joins in week three. Sharing to thirty is the same work as sharing to three.

Practice papers, written for you

Give it the topic, level and difficulty; the AI drafts the questions and the answer key. You edit anything you like — nothing reaches a student until you publish it.

Fees without the spreadsheet

Fee on the batch, overridable per student, invoiced per cycle and tracked to paid — with a reminder you send by WhatsApp or SMS in one tap. We track and chase; we don't collect.

Fees stay yours alone

Bring in a teacher and they genuinely cannot read your fee data — a permission enforced in the database, not a screen we hide.

Phone or laptop

Run your practice from the app today. Browser access — the same practice, the same sign-in, on a laptop — is nearly ready, because an evening of invoicing is easier with a keyboard.

That's the core of it, and it all works today. We're deliberately not listing everything else yet — we're still shaping this with the tutors using it, and we'd rather change our minds in a conversation with you than defend a feature list. Ask us about anything that isn't here.

AI, and where we draw the line

It writes your papers. It is not given your children.

Plenty of education apps have bolted AI on and quietly started feeding student records into it. We took the opposite decision, and it's worth being precise about what that means.

Sent to the AI

Only what you typed
  • The topic
  • The subject
  • The difficulty
  • How many questions

Never leaves Planur

Everything about a child
  • Student names
  • Results and attendance
  • Fees and invoices
  • Parent names and contacts
  • Your uploaded materials

Four short pieces of text go out. Nothing about a child does — and that isn't a policy we wrote down, it's the whole of what the app sends. No provider we use trains on it, and we re-check that rather than assuming it stays true.

The fair question

You'd be handing us your roster, your register and your fees.

Rather than asking for faith, here is what's already decided and enforced — a layer below the app, where a bug in a screen can't undo it.

Those rules are covered by an automated test suite that tries to break them against the real database before anything ships. And if you stop paying, nothing is deleted — you simply can't add more until you're back on a plan.
